**Bachelor's Degree Thesis Title: Analyzing the Perceived Intention to Adopt Cryptocurrency using Integrated Technology Readiness and Acceptance Model: A PLS-SEM Model**

Despite advances in blockchain-based technologies, university students, a key demographic for future technology integration, are slow to adopt cryptocurrency,
highlighting the need to understand adoption barriers. Amidst extensive research on technology acceptance, the complex relationships between technology readiness,
acceptance factors, and perceived risk in cryptocurrency adoption among Filipino university students are poorly understood. This quantitative, cross-sectional study used
Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) to analyze Visayas State University students' perceived intention to adopt cryptocurrency using an
Integrated Technology Readiness and Acceptance Model (TRAM) in the school year of 2024–2025. This study found that young, female university students, mostly from
Management and Economics, have high digital comfort and internet engagement. They showed emerging financial proactivity despite receiving family allowances. In terms of
the interrelationships among the latent variables, the study found that technology readiness (optimism and innovativeness) positively affected technology acceptance
(perceived ease of use and usefulness), which also reduced perceived risk. However, some direct effects, such as innovativeness' effect on perceived usefulness and
perceived usefulness's effect on perceived risk and behavioral intention, were not supported, suggesting that novelty can overshadow utility and that digital natives'
usefulness and risk are intertwined. Finally, only the faculty affiliation among the control variables significantly affected behavioral intention to use cryptocurrency. This
highlights the role of financial education and market exposure in adoption, leading to Philippine policy recommendations for specialized crypto education and consumer
protection.
